\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The results show that topology and resource reuse cannot be separated when interpreting dense aerial sidelink. Prior multi-UAV work likewise couples interference with geometry, routing, and frequency allocation \cite{lau2023outage,alam2024jtfr}; recent U2U sidelink work also demonstrates that MCS adaptation materially affects throughput/reliability trade-offs \cite{yu2025uavlink}. Our controls show the same dependence: resource partitioning helps the long-link baseline, but shortening desired links changes the shared-resource regime more strongly.

Orthogonalization is not free. The $R=2$ versus $R=4$ result reflects the joint effect of conflict-graph placement, lower co-channel interference, and reduced TBS as PRBs are split. Accordingly, the operating envelope is a map for one explicit topology/load/model combination rather than a scalar maximum swarm size.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
\emph{Propagation domain:} the fitted A2A law comes from a 3.5~GHz, 50~MHz, 30~dBm campaign with both drones at 100~m, an initial separation of 85~m, and a roughly 1~km A2A flight path in a mostly hilly/forested, rural-like campus environment \cite{erdemir2023a2a}. Our carrier, bandwidth, altitude, and transmit power match those conditions, but the synthetic square allows separations up to 1.41~km; the longest simulated links therefore extrapolate beyond the reported A2A flight path. Shadowing correlation, fast fading, blockage, Doppler, antenna-body effects, and temporal channel memory are omitted.

\emph{PHY/MAC abstraction:} the study uses 3GPP-derived MCS/TBS/LDPC rules but an explicit simplified resource grid. PSFCH, SL-PRS, detailed SCI resource accounting, feedback timing, retransmissions, power control, and standards-complete sensing/resource selection are outside the paper model. Different abstract frequency resources are perfectly orthogonal. The 5G-LENA curves are generic NR EESM link-level tables \cite{patriciello2019e2e,ferreira2026lena}; requested code-block size is mapped to the nearest sourced same-MCS/base-graph curve, SINR is linearly interpolated and endpoint-clamped, and TB-BLER uses an independent-code-block approximation. These choices bound the absolute goodput interpretation.

\emph{Traffic/topology abstraction:} each snapshot contains $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ simultaneously active one-way links; it does not model bidirectional application traffic, queues, mobility during a packet exchange, or mission-constrained partner selection. Greedy short-link pairing is a sensitivity control, not a proposed association protocol. Likewise, $G$ is a desired/interferer power-ratio sensitivity, not an antenna-array or beam-tracking model.

\emph{Statistics and policy:} matched seeds reduce geometry variance in treatment comparisons, but the operating-envelope thresholds are engineering choices applied to scenario means. They do not require the lower confidence bound to satisfy the target and therefore should not be interpreted as reliability guarantees.

\subsection{Design implications}
Within these boundaries, four conclusions are supported by the evaluated model: (i) desired-link topology must be specified before interpreting density scaling; (ii) more frequency groups do not necessarily improve goodput because interference reduction competes with PRB/TBS loss; (iii) geometry-aware reuse outperforms random allocation in the tested cases; and (iv) link adaptation and spatial selectivity complement rather than replace topology/resource design. Future work should repeat the matched-seed methodology with standards-complete sidelink sensing/scheduling, richer channel dynamics, realistic bidirectional traffic, and multi-UAV RF validation.
