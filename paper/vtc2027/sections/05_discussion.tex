\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The main implication is that dense aerial sidelink should jointly control \emph{who communicates with whom} and \emph{how resources are reused}. Prior multi-UAV work likewise couples interference with geometry, routing, and frequency allocation \cite{lau2023outage,alam2024jtfr}, while UAV-sidelink studies motivate communication-aware scheduling \cite{mishra2022cu2x}. Our robustness results sharpen this point: resource isolation helps the long-link baseline, but nearest-neighbor pairing changes the shared-resource regime more strongly by shortening desired links.

Orthogonalization is not free. The $R=2$ versus $R=4$ result shows that fewer interferers can increase SINR while narrower partitions reduce TBS and goodput. Hence, a scheduler should balance reuse and payload bandwidth rather than maximize orthogonality. Likewise, a single maximum swarm size is not meaningful without a pairing rule, area/density model, activity level, and scheduling policy.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
Geometry is the dominant external-validity limitation. In the fixed $1000\times1000$~m area, sequential pairing leaves desired-link length roughly unchanged as $N$ grows, whereas nearest-neighbor pairing shortens local links. UAV literature similarly emphasizes altitude, mobility, LoS probability, and deployment geometry as key determinants of channel and interference behavior \cite{zeng2019sky,mozaffari2019tutorial}. The reported curves therefore describe explicit topology families, not all swarm organizations.

The channel model is large-scale and equal-altitude. Although anchored in measured A2A data \cite{erdemir2023a2a}, it omits correlated shadowing, fast fading, blockage, and trajectory-dependent memory; Doppler and synchronization are also recognized UAV-sidelink concerns \cite{zhang2026sync}. These effects may alter outage tails.

The PHY/MAC model is also deliberately abstract. 5G-LENA BLER curves are simulation evidence \cite{patriciello2019e2e,ferreira2026lena}, not standardized BLER requirements. Adaptive MCS assumes immediate modeled-SINR knowledge, and the fixed-MCS control shows that this assumption matters. Standard NR sidelink defines explicit resource-allocation procedures \cite{garcia2021nrv2x,3gpp38300}, whereas our conflict graph is a geometry proxy; $G$ is a relative-interference sensitivity, not a beam codebook or measured antenna gain.

\subsection{Design implications}
Within these boundaries, four conclusions are stable: topology must be specified before claiming swarm capacity; orthogonalization should be selective because SINR gain competes with TBS loss; geometry-aware reuse improves upon random allocation; and spatial selectivity/link adaptation complement rather than replace topology and resource design. A practical design could therefore maintain short communication neighborhoods, estimate local conflict, choose a reuse level, and then adapt spatial selectivity and MCS to residual interference. Future work should replace these controlled abstractions with standards-complete sidelink sensing/scheduling, fading and mobility, and multi-UAV RF validation.
