\section{Introduction}
Reliable wireless links are a core enabling technology for cooperative UAV systems, whose mobility and altitude create propagation, interference, and networking conditions that differ substantially from terrestrial deployments \cite{zeng2019sky,mozaffari2019tutorial}. NR sidelink provides standardized direct-communication mechanisms \cite{garcia2021nrv2x} and is therefore a relevant candidate for intra-swarm communication, as already explored in UAV-swarm scheduling and suitability studies \cite{mishra2022cu2x,varonen2024sidelink}.

Aerial line-of-sight can strengthen both the desired link and many simultaneous interferers. Consequently, increasing $N$ can push a shared resource toward aggregate-interference domination, but $N$ alone is not sufficient to predict the regime: the desired-link geometry induced by the communication topology can materially change the SINR distribution. Interference mitigation is also cross-layer. Frequency-resource isolation reduces co-channel interference but simultaneously reduces per-link PRBs and transport-block size, while spatial selectivity changes the desired/interferer power ratio without adding bandwidth.

Prior work covers sidelink scheduling, analytical U2U outage, joint trajectory/frequency/routing optimization, topology control, MCS adaptation, and synchronization \cite{lau2023outage,alam2024jtfr,bai2026topology,yu2025uavlink,zhang2026sync}. What remains insufficiently characterized is their intersection: a controlled NR-aware operating-region study that conditions density effects on communication topology while explicitly charging resource isolation for its PRB/TBS cost.

This paper asks: \emph{How do communication topology and swarm density jointly determine the evaluated UAV-sidelink interference regime, and when do bandwidth-aware resource isolation and spatial desired/interferer advantage expand the resulting reliability/goodput operating region?} The contributions are threefold. First, we combine measurement-derived A2A path loss with 3GPP-derived MCS/TBS/LDPC mechanics under an explicit study resource grid and sourced 5G-LENA link-level BLER curves. Second, matched-seed sequential versus greedy short-link pairing establishes topology as a first-order scaling variable in the evaluated model. Third, we quantify the resource-isolation trade-off and joint operating envelope while varying allocator, link adaptation, and noise-bandwidth accounting. The paper is intentionally system-level and does not claim a standards-complete sidelink PHY/MAC or measured multi-UAV RF performance.
