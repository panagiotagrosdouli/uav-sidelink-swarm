\section{Introduction}
Reliable wireless links are a core enabling technology for cooperative UAV systems, whose mobility and altitude create propagation, interference, and networking conditions that differ substantially from conventional terrestrial deployments \cite{zeng2019sky,mozaffari2019tutorial}. NR sidelink provides standardized direct-communication and resource-allocation mechanisms \cite{garcia2021nrv2x}. This makes sidelink a relevant candidate for intra-swarm communication, as investigated in UAV-swarm scheduling and suitability studies \cite{mishra2022cu2x,varonen2024sidelink}.

Aerial links frequently exhibit strong line-of-sight components, which can strengthen both desired and interfering signals \cite{zeng2019sky,mozaffari2019tutorial}. Analytical multi-UAV work shows that node geometry and co-channel interference materially affect SINR and outage \cite{lau2023outage}. Related studies jointly optimize trajectory, frequency, and routing \cite{alam2024jtfr}, adapt MCS for U2U sidelink \cite{yu2025uavlink}, or address synchronization and Doppler-aware sidelink access \cite{zhang2026sync}. We therefore examine how desired-link topology interacts with the bandwidth cost of resource isolation when reliability is evaluated through NR-aware transport-block mechanics rather than a single SINR threshold.

This paper asks: \emph{How do swarm density and communication topology change the evaluated NR-sidelink interference regime, and how far can resource separation and spatial desired/interferer advantage move the resulting reliability/goodput operating region?} The contributions are threefold. First, we combine measurement-derived A2A path loss with 3GPP-derived MCS/TBS/LDPC mechanics under an explicit study resource grid and sourced 5G-LENA link-level BLER curves. Second, we quantify the trade-off between co-channel interference reduction and reduced per-link PRBs when the 133-PRB profile is partitioned into 1/2/4/8 resources. Third, matched-seed controls vary pairing, allocator, link adaptation, and noise-bandwidth accounting to identify which conclusions are robust and which are model-dependent. In particular, the severe high-density degradation of the long-link baseline is not topology invariant.
