\section{Introduction}
Reliable wireless links are a core enabling technology for cooperative UAV systems, whose mobility and altitude create propagation, interference, and networking conditions that differ substantially from conventional terrestrial deployments \cite{zeng2019sky,mozaffari2019tutorial}. NR sidelink provides standardized direct communication mechanisms and resource-allocation procedures without requiring every user-plane transmission to traverse the cellular infrastructure \cite{garcia2021nrv2x,3gpp38300}. This makes sidelink a relevant candidate for intra-swarm communication, as also investigated directly in UAV-swarm studies \cite{mishra2022cu2x,varonen2024sidelink}.

Aerial links frequently exhibit strong line-of-sight components, which can strengthen the desired signal but also increase the number and strength of visible interferers \cite{zeng2019sky,mozaffari2019tutorial}. Analytical multi-UAV work has shown that UAV-to-UAV interference and node geometry can strongly affect SINR and outage \cite{lau2023outage}. Related studies have considered joint trajectory/frequency/routing optimization \cite{alam2024jtfr}, site-specific unmanned-platform sidelink \cite{giannakoulas2025uxu}, and synchronization/Doppler-aware sidelink access \cite{zhang2026sync}. What remains less explicit is how communication topology interacts with the bandwidth cost of resource isolation when reliability is evaluated through NR-aware transport-block mechanics rather than a single SINR threshold.

This paper asks: \emph{How do swarm density and communication topology change the NR sidelink interference regime, and how far can resource separation and directional spatial selectivity extend the feasible reliability/goodput region?} The contributions are threefold. First, we combine measurement-derived A2A propagation with standards-based NR transport-block mechanics and sourced link-level BLER curves. Second, we quantify an exact PRB-partition operating envelope under a controlled full-load baseline. Third, we test whether the headline conclusions survive changes in pairing, resource allocator, link adaptation, and noise-bandwidth accounting. This last step is essential: the severe density collapse observed under arbitrary long desired links is not topology invariant.
