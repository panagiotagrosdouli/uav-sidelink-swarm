\section{Related Work and Research Gap}
UAV-communications surveys identify line-of-sight propagation, three-dimensional mobility, interference, and topology as central design variables \cite{zeng2019sky,mozaffari2019tutorial}; the NR-V2X literature documents sidelink physical-layer and autonomous resource-selection mechanisms \cite{garcia2021nrv2x}. UAV-specific work uses sidelink for communication-aware scheduling and multi-hop forwarding \cite{mishra2022cu2x}, evaluates NR sidelink for internal swarm communication \cite{varonen2024sidelink}, and studies dynamic U2U MCS control in ns-3 \cite{yu2025uavlink}. Complementary work derives multi-UAV interference/outage models \cite{lau2023outage}, jointly optimizes trajectory, frequency, and routing \cite{alam2024jtfr}, or targets synchronization and Doppler-aware sidelink access \cite{zhang2026sync}.

Our study does not claim a new standards-complete sidelink protocol. Instead, it isolates swarm size, desired-link topology, frequency-resource partitioning, a geometry-based reuse allocator, and an experimental spatial desired/interferer advantage while retaining NR-derived TBS/LDPC mechanics and sourced link-level BLER behavior. The key distinction is the matched-seed robustness layer: pairing, random versus geometry-aware allocation, adaptive versus fixed MCS, and noise-bandwidth accounting are varied explicitly rather than absorbed into a single optimized policy.
