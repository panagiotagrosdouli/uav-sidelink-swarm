\section{Related Work and Research Gap}
UAV-communications surveys identify line-of-sight propagation, three-dimensional mobility, interference, and topology as central design variables \cite{zeng2019sky,mozaffari2019tutorial}; the NR-V2X literature documents sidelink physical-layer and autonomous resource-selection mechanisms \cite{garcia2021nrv2x}. UAV-specific work uses sidelink for communication-aware scheduling and multi-hop forwarding \cite{mishra2022cu2x}, evaluates NR sidelink for internal swarm communication \cite{varonen2024sidelink}, and studies dynamic U2U MCS control in ns-3 \cite{yu2025uavlink}. Complementary work derives multi-UAV interference/outage models \cite{lau2023outage}, jointly optimizes trajectory, frequency, and routing \cite{alam2024jtfr}, or targets synchronization and Doppler-aware sidelink access \cite{zhang2026sync}.

Topology itself is also an active optimization variable. Bai et al. jointly optimize UAV connection relationships, transmit power, traffic, and positions under mutual interference \cite{bai2026topology}. We therefore do not claim that topology control is new. The narrower gap addressed here is an NR-aware, matched-seed characterization of how desired-link topology changes the density/interference regime and how exact resource isolation behaves once its reduced PRB/TBS budget is propagated through link adaptation and BLER mapping.

Our study accordingly does not propose a standards-complete sidelink protocol. It isolates swarm size, desired-link topology, frequency-resource partitioning, a geometry-based reuse allocator, and an experimental spatial desired/interferer advantage. Pairing, random versus geometry-aware allocation, adaptive versus fixed MCS, and noise-bandwidth accounting are varied explicitly so that model-sensitive conclusions are exposed rather than hidden inside a single optimized policy.
