\section{Related Work and Research Gap}
The broader UAV-communications literature identifies line-of-sight dominance, three-dimensional mobility, interference, and topology as central design variables \cite{zeng2019sky,mozaffari2019tutorial}. For sidelink specifically, the NR-V2X literature documents the standardized sidelink physical layer, resource allocation, QoS, and direct-communication procedures that motivate reuse of the air interface beyond road vehicles \cite{garcia2021nrv2x,3gpp38300}.

Within UAV swarms, Mishra \emph{et al.} use 5G sidelink for cooperative communication with communication-aware scheduling and multi-hop forwarding \cite{mishra2022cu2x}, while Varonen evaluates NR sidelink suitability and routing-update requirements for internal swarm communication \cite{varonen2024sidelink}. Lau \emph{et al.} derive a general analytical U2U outage model that explicitly captures interference for arbitrary multi-UAV node locations \cite{lau2023outage}. Alam and Moh jointly optimize trajectory, frequency allocation, and routing through multi-agent reinforcement learning \cite{alam2024jtfr}. Giannakoulas \emph{et al.} investigate sidelink for unmanned-platform swarms in challenging, site-specific propagation scenarios \cite{giannakoulas2025uxu}, and Zhang \emph{et al.} focus on synchronization and Doppler-aware sidelink access \cite{zhang2026sync}.

These studies establish the relevance of sidelink, interference, topology, and resource control, but they answer different questions. Our goal is not to propose a new routing or learning optimizer. Instead, we isolate four coupled variables---swarm size, desired-link topology, exact frequency-resource partitioning, and spatial desired/interferer advantage---while retaining NR-aware TBS/LDPC and sourced BLER behavior. The robustness layer is equally important: it checks whether conclusions attributed to density actually survive different pairings, allocators, link adaptation, and noise accounting.
