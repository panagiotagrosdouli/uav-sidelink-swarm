\section{Conclusion}
In the evaluated full-load sequential-disjoint topology, increasing swarm size drives shared-resource sidelink into a severe aggregate-interference regime, while exact resource partitioning and directional relative advantage substantially expand the feasible region. However, the 100-seed robustness campaign shows that topology is first-order: at $N=100$, nearest-neighbor pairing raises shared-resource success from 0.0128 to 0.4766 by shortening desired links. Geometry-aware allocation and adaptive MCS provide additional gains, whereas exact PRB noise-bandwidth accounting is negligible in the evaluated interference regimes. The resulting design message is therefore topology-aware: dense UAV sidelink should jointly manage communication pairing, resource reuse, spatial selectivity, and link adaptation.
