\begin{abstract}
Direct NR sidelink can support infrastructure-independent UAV-swarm coordination, but its interference regime depends jointly on density, communication topology, resource reuse, and spatial selectivity. We present a reproducible system-level study that combines a published 3.5~GHz air-to-air path-loss fit, 3GPP-derived MCS/TBS/LDPC mechanics under an explicit simplified sidelink resource-grid profile, and generic NR link-level EESM BLER curves processed from the official 5G-LENA v5.0 release. For swarm size $N$, $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ one-way disjoint links are active per snapshot. A 100-seed campaign evaluates 5--100 UAVs, partitioning a 133-PRB 50~MHz/30~kHz profile into 1/2/4/8 abstract orthogonal resources and sweeping a 0--9~dB desired/interferer relative advantage. Under the full-load sequential-disjoint baseline, $N=100$ yields $-23.03$~dB mean SINR, 0.0128 first-transmission success probability, and 0.359~Mbps expected PHY goodput; $R=8,G=6$ raises the latter metrics to 0.5292 and 2.229~Mbps. A separate 100-seed robustness campaign shows that topology is a first-order determinant in this model: greedy short-link pairing at $N=100,R=1,G=0$ reduces mean desired-link length from 517~m to 84~m and raises success probability to 0.4766. The results therefore characterize topology-dependent, mean-based policy operating regions rather than a universal swarm-capacity limit.
\end{abstract}
