\begin{abstract}
Direct NR sidelink can support infrastructure-independent UAV-swarm coordination, but its interference regime depends jointly on density, communication topology, resource reuse, and spatial selectivity. We present a reproducible system-level study combining a published 3.5~GHz air-to-air path-loss fit, standards-based NR MCS/TBS/LDPC mechanics, and official 5G-LENA v5.0 SINR-to-BLER data. A 100-seed baseline campaign evaluates 5--100 UAVs, exact partitioning of a 133-PRB 50~MHz/30~kHz profile into 1/2/4/8 resources, and a 0--9~dB experimental desired/interferer directional advantage. Under the full-load sequential-disjoint baseline, 100 UAVs yield $-23.03$~dB mean SINR, 0.0128 first-transmission success, and 0.359~Mbps expected PHY goodput; $R=8,G=6$ raises these to 0.5292 success and 2.229~Mbps. A separate 100-seed robustness campaign shows that topology is first-order: nearest-neighbor pairing at $N=100,R=1,G=0$ shortens the mean desired link from 517~m to 84~m and raises success to 0.4766. The results therefore characterize topology-dependent operating regions, not a universal swarm-capacity limit.
\end{abstract}
